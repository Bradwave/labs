# Fourier Lab XR

Prototype **augmented-reality (WebXR) version of Fourier Lab**: wind a signal around the frequency axis, grab the handle, slide it along the axis, and the centre of mass `c(f)` draws the Fourier transform as a trail in the room. Starting point: the single-file prototype "Fourier Lab" (artifact, 2026-11-13; its info dialog already promised "la mano afferra la maniglia, il polso fa girare la manopola e il centro di massa lascia una scia nello spazio"). Read `docs/PLAN.md` first, then `../DESIGN.md` §17 (XR apps). Conventions follow `../force-lab-xr`: static site, no build, Three.js **r128** (`js/three.min.js`, global `THREE`), no remote resources (headsets are offline), code comments in Italian, UI strings in Italian **and** English (`js/strings.js`, same keys). Always say what is **unverified on the Quest**.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `js/fourier.js` | **pure** (UMD, global `Fourier`): signal `g(t)`, `Wheel` (flywheel: clockwise-only ratchet, friction, f = κ·w), `Model` (centre of mass `c(f)`, spectrum, peaks, explored runs, `wound(f)` points), knob angle maths (`knobAngle`, `crankDelta`), dial maths (`dialPhi`, `dialAngle`, `dialTurn`, `HZ_PER_TURN` = 8), `products`, `walk`, `phasors`, `signalSamples` (for the advanced views and the sound), prediction flags (`evalFlags`, not yet used in the UI). Same maths as the 2D prototype. |
| `js/audio.js` | Web Audio engine `RadioAudio.Engine` (classic script): the signal buffer looped at T/K s plus an oscillator at f·K (K = `Fourier.AUDIO_K` = 110); modes `sum` (beats), `mixer` (product, via a gain whose value is the oscillator), `probe` (tone with loudness |c|). Starts only after a user gesture. Not testable in Node. |
| `js/grab.js` | copied unchanged from `force-lab-xr` (two-hand grab and scale of the stage; pinch hysteresis). |
| `js/app.js` | THREE scene (`stage`), instanced thick-line segments, labels, floating canvas panel, hand/controller input (`xrInput`), desktop camera and drag, WebXR sessions. Test hook `window.__app`. |
| `js/main.js`, `js/strings.js` | kit setup and strings. |
| `sw.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, icons | generated: `node tools/sync-kit.mjs fourier-lab-xr`, `node tools/logos.mjs fourier-lab-xr` (from `logo.svg`). |

Frames: `stage` (movable and scalable with the base cube; default 0.28 m per unit, so the f axis is about 1 m long) holds axis, winding plane, ring and bead, handle, wound signal, centre of mass and trace. X = f, Y = Im, Z = Re. The winding turns clockwise: `(Re, Im) = g(t)·(cos 2πft, −sin 2πft)`. Interaction maths runs in stage coordinates (`stage.worldToLocal`), so moving or scaling the stage never changes what a gesture means.

The signal is a physical **rope** (`woundM`): at `S.u = 0` it hangs on a time rail behind the f axis (x = 0.2 + 0.9·t, z = −2.2, ticks in seconds) as the plain graph g(t); turning f twists it into a corkscrew (θ = 2πft around the rail); **Wind** (`ACT.wind`, key W, panel button) animates `S.u` to 1 over 1.6 s, sliding the rope into the Re–Im plane where it is the wound curve. The centre of mass dot is the centroid of the rope, always visible: on the rail it sits off the time axis by c(f) rotated by the wind angle, and it slides with the rope into the plane where it equals c(f); the trace (the transform) and the ghost are always visible, and a second dot `tipDot` (label c(f)) always sits at the tip of the trace, at (f·KX, Im c, Re c) with a vector from the axis; it follows f. Yellow beads mark t = 0…4 s on the rope. Note: in this r128 build `setColorAt` sizes its buffer from `mesh.count`, so set `count` before painting instance colours.

Controls: **handle** (yellow box on the axis) = pinch or trigger/grip near it, hand position along the axis is `f`; **bead** on the ring = grab and turn the wrist clockwise around the axis (`Fourier.crankDelta`, 1 turn = 1 Hz); **base cube** = grab to move the scene, second hand pinching anywhere scales it; **panel** (floating card) = index fingertip poke, or controller ray + trigger: Sweep, Reset, Preset, Whole curve, Plane, Recentre, and a click/drag on the spectrum plot sets `f`. **Wheel** (Input → Wheel, or the panel's Wheel button): a spoked disc left of the axis in the Re–Im plane; grab the rim and throw it clockwise, f = κ·turns/s (κ = 1, 2, 4, 8 from the panel's κ button or the page), friction slows it, a still hand or Brake stops it; handle and knob are off in this mode. On screen: drag the handle, orbit, wheel/pinch to zoom, slider, Space / ← → / R.

The palette is the Fourier Lab prototype's (sand, orange accent), recorded in `apps.json` as proposed. In the headset the dark palette is used (readable in VR black and in passthrough).

Tests: `node tests/fourier.test.mjs`. Headless XR checks: in the page, set `__app.SRC[i]` (`kind`, `active`), fake `__app.hands[i].joints`, call `__app.xrInput(now, dt)` (it needs `renderer.xr.isPresenting` only for the camera in `placeFromHead`).

**Input modes** (`S.input`): `direct` (handle, knob, slider), `wheel` (flywheel), `radio`. **Radio**: a dial face left of the axis facing the user (`radioG`, centre `RD`), a tuning knob (270° = 0–6 Hz, 8 Hz per turn, clicks every 0.25 Hz), a |c| meter, a reference oscillator turning at f/4 rev/s; hands/controllers turn the knob with `Fourier.dialAngle/dialTurn`, controllers buzz in proportion to |c|; on screen drag around the knob. Entering radio switches the sound to `sum` if it was off. Sound is a separate setting (`S.sound`: off, sum, mixer, probe) that works in every mode.

**Advanced options** (`S.adv`, saved under `fourier-lab-xr.adv`; page: the "Advanced" section, headset: panel page 2 via "Advanced"): `walk` (partial integral path in the winding plane, scaled so a perfect match ends near the ring), `phase` (trace coloured by arg c), `shadows` (Re(f) on a floor plane, Im(f) on a back wall), `epi` (epicycles of the top peaks, real part scrolls), `plot` (signal plot shows g(t), the product g·cos with its signed area, or g against cos with the 2 Re c fit line). Not done: resonator bank, diffraction, spectrogram.

Pitfalls found: in this r128 build `setColorAt` sizes its buffer from `mesh.count`, and the instance-colour buffer must already exist when the shader is first compiled (create it even with zero segments), otherwise colours are ignored.
