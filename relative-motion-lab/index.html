How it works

One object moves. Two observers describe it: S is the fixed frame, S′ moves relative to S. If S′ only translates (its axes stay parallel to those of S), the classical rules hold:

  • Δr = Δr′ + ΔR
  • v = v′ + V
  • a = a′ + A

R, V, A are the position, velocity and acceleration of the origin O′ measured in S.

Colours and arrows

Each panel’s own grid is solid; the other frame’s grid is dashed orange and slides past. Velocity arrows use the same scale in both panels, and so do accelerations. Positions use each panel’s zoom unless you turn on same scale.

Predict, then reveal

Press Predict. The panel of the frame you have to work out hides the object. Draw the path you expect with your finger or mouse, then press Reveal: the motion replays and your sketch stays on top for comparison.

Rotating frames

In Set up, “Rotation of S′” makes S′ turn with angular velocity Ω. Then extra terms appear: v = V + v′ + Ω×r′ and a = A + a′ + 2Ω×v′ − Ω²r′ (Coriolis and centripetal terms). In the Vectors tab, v′ and a′ are drawn in the axes of S so the arrows can be added. The merry-go-round and turntable scenarios use this.

Setting up your own

Choose how S′ moves, then how the object moves and in which frame you describe it. Switching “given in” keeps the numbers but changes their meaning, so the motion changes.
